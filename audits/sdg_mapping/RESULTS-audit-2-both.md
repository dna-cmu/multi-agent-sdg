# Audit 2 — SDG mapping, both auditors, before resolution

120 claims: 100 that the framework mapped to at least one goal and 20 that it left unmapped (excluded from the corpus scores). Each auditor listed up to three goals per claim, or 0 for none, from the claim text and section title. EE gave targets; her goal is the number before the point. Intervals are Wilson 95%.

## 1. Agreement between the two auditors
- Same first goal (or both none): 99 of 120 (82%; 75–88%).
- At least one goal in common (or both none): 105 of 120 (88%; 80–92%).
- Claim × goal cells (120 × 17 = 2,040): DNA lists 173, EE lists 133, both 120; agreement 96.8%, Cohen’s κ 0.77.
- Whether the claim addresses any SDG: same answer for 107 of 120 (89%; 82–94%); DNA says none for 16, EE for 27.

## 3. Target level (EE only; DNA gave goals)
- Framework target assignments that EE also lists: 118 of 496 (24%; 20–28%); claims with at least one target in common: 79 of 100 (79%; 70–86%). EE could list at most three targets and the framework lists 5.0 per claim on average, so this is a lower bound on target-level precision.

## Notes
- The auditors could list at most three goals; the framework assigns one to three goals to these claims, so the limit does not by itself lower precision.
- Goal-level precision here means the auditor independently named the same goal, which is stricter than judging a shown claim–goal pair as acceptable (the wording of the pre-specified measure). The form asked for independent listing so that missed goals could be measured.
